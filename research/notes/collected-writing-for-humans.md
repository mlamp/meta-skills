## Writing for humans

PR bodies, commit messages, and doc prose are read by people:

- Open with a plain sentence stating what changed.
- One idea per sentence; avoid long em-dash chains.
- References (ids, section anchors, links) go at the end of a sentence or in a trailing block, not mid-clause.
- Order by reader priority: what and why first, mechanism after.
- List touched files as bullets, not prose.
- Concise is not dense — prefer short sentences over packed ones.
- Natural voice, to a person — people and actions, not abstractions.

## Evidence discipline

Name a cause only when data shows it; label hypotheses; never claim "it works" without the command, output, and exit status; when data is missing, say "unknown" and list what to record. Already global rules in the user's setup — the contract restates only what the target project lacks.

## Drive (anti-pause)

The practice holds that current models carry a trained pause bias: mid-task offramps ("should I continue?", "want me to proceed?") that stall multi-step work. Counter-rules: a framed multi-step task is one slice, executed to completion; questions only for genuine sub-decisions after self-investigation; the user is the interrupter. The mechanism claim arrives unproven here.
